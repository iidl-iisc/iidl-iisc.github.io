Research

Advancing thermofluid systems through experimental investigation

My research program focuses on experimental investigations of combustion systems and fluid dynamics phenomena with applications in sustainable energy technologies. I employ advanced diagnostic techniques including high-speed imaging, laser-based measurements, and pressure transducers to probe complex thermofluid systems and understand fundamental mechanisms governing their behavior.

01

Experimental Investigation of Novel Combustor Concepts for Fuel Flexibility and Reduced Emissions

This research thrust focuses on developing and characterizing advanced combustion systems capable of operating with diverse fuel compositions while minimizing pollutant formation. The work addresses critical challenges in transitioning to sustainable energy systems, where combustors must accommodate varying fuel properties including hydrogen-enriched natural gas, biogas, and other alternative fuels.

Key Research Questions

  • How do different fuel compositions affect flame stabilization mechanisms and combustion efficiency?
  • What combustor geometries and operating conditions enable stable operation across a wide fuel flexibility envelope?
  • How can we minimize NOx and CO emissions while maintaining fuel flexibility?
  • What are the fundamental interactions between swirl-induced flow structures and flame dynamics in multi-fuel combustors?

Approach & Methods

I utilize swirl-stabilized combustor configurations with systematic variation of fuel composition, bulk velocity, and equivalence ratio. Experimental diagnostics include high-speed chemiluminescence imaging to capture flame structure and dynamics, acoustic pressure measurements to characterize thermoacoustic behavior, and exhaust gas analysis for emissions quantification.

Current Work

Recent investigations have examined frequency shifting behavior in thermoacoustic oscillations when transitioning between pure methane and hydrogen-methane blends. This work reveals complex coupling between acoustic modes and flame macrostructure that varies significantly with fuel composition, providing insights for developing robust combustor designs.

02

Experimental Study of Nonlinear Hydrodynamics Instability and Control

Hydrodynamic instabilities play a crucial role in numerous engineering systems, from combustion devices to heat exchangers. This research investigates the fundamental mechanisms governing instability onset, growth, and saturation, with emphasis on developing effective control strategies. Understanding nonlinear behavior is essential for predicting system dynamics and implementing robust control approaches.

Key Research Questions

  • What are the dominant instability mechanisms in swirl-stabilized flows and combustion systems?
  • How do nonlinear interactions between different instability modes affect system behavior?
  • Can we develop predictive models for instability transitions based on experimental observations?
  • What control strategies are most effective for suppressing or mitigating instabilities?

Approach & Methods

Experimental investigations employ time-resolved measurements including high-speed imaging and multi-point pressure acquisition to capture spatio-temporal evolution of instabilities. Modal decomposition techniques and phase-space reconstruction methods reveal underlying dynamical structures. Control approaches include both passive strategies (geometric modifications) and active methods (forced perturbations).

Research Directions

Future work will explore machine learning approaches for real-time instability detection and adaptive control, development of reduced-order models for instability prediction, and investigation of instability interactions in multi-physics systems including coupled thermoacoustic-hydrodynamic phenomena.

03

Levitating Micro-droplets on Non-wetting Surfaces

The behavior of droplets on superheated non-wetting surfaces exhibits fascinating physics with important implications for heat transfer, spray cooling, and droplet manipulation. When a liquid droplet contacts a surface significantly above its boiling point, a vapor layer can form between the droplet and surface, causing the droplet to levitate—the Leidenfrost effect. This research explores the fundamental physics of levitating droplets and their potential applications.

Key Research Questions

  • How do surface texture and chemistry affect droplet levitation dynamics and stability?
  • What controls the vapor layer thickness and heat transfer characteristics?
  • Can we manipulate levitating droplets for controlled transport or mixing applications?
  • How does droplet composition affect levitation behavior and evaporation dynamics?

Approach & Methods

Experimental studies employ high-speed imaging to capture droplet oscillations, shape deformation, and motion. Infrared thermography reveals surface temperature distributions and vapor layer dynamics. Precise control of surface temperature, droplet size, and fluid properties enables systematic investigation of parameter space.

Potential Applications

This research has implications for spray cooling in high heat-flux applications, microfluidic devices requiring contactless droplet manipulation, and fundamental understanding of boiling heat transfer. Future directions include investigating multi-droplet interactions, exploring chemical reactions within levitating droplets, and developing applications in materials synthesis.

Research Philosophy

My research approach emphasizes rigorous experimental methods combined with physical insight to advance fundamental understanding while addressing practical engineering challenges. I believe in the power of careful experimentation to reveal new phenomena and guide development of predictive models. Collaboration across disciplines—from fundamental fluid mechanics to applied combustion engineering—enables comprehensive investigation of complex thermofluid systems.

I am committed to training the next generation of researchers in experimental techniques, critical thinking, and scientific communication. My future research program will provide students with hands-on experience in state-of-the-art experimental facilities while fostering their development as independent researchers capable of advancing knowledge in thermofluid sciences.
