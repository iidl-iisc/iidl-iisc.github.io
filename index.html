About

The Instability and Interfacial Dynamics Laboratory (IIDL) is part of the Department of Mechanical Engineering at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore, and is headed by Dr. Abhijit Kumar Kushwaha.

The major focus of lab is investigating the fundamental physics governing fluid instabilities, combustion dynamics, and droplet-surface interactions. Our research bridges classical fluid mechanics and cutting-edge experimental diagnostics to uncover the nonlinear phenomena that underlie complex engineering systems.

At IIDL, our research works spans a broad spectrum-from the control of combustion-driven instabilities and open-loop manipulation of globally unstable flows to micro-Newton-scale friction measurements and droplet dynamics on non-wetting (superhydrophobic and superheated) surfaces. We view these diverse topics as interconnected components of a larger physical framework that links fluid mechanics across scales and applications. Our research aims to advance both fundamental understanding and practical design strategies in microfluidics, aerospace, biomedical systems, and industrial fluid transport.

We combine experimental rigor with modern data analysis to extract physical insight from rich datasets.

IIDL welcomes curious, motivated students and researchers eager to work at the frontier of experimental fluid mechanics.

Research Focus Areas

01

Hydrodynamic Instabilities

Experimental study of nonlinear dynamics and vortex dynamics of globally unstable jets and their control strategies

02

Droplet Dynamics, Electrospray, and Encapsulations

Unraveling the dynamics of micro-droplets on non-wetting surfaces for advanced applications

03

Nature Inspired Engineering

Experimental study of bio-inspired designs for improving the aerodynamic performance of aerofoils and drag-reducing through the surface modification by microfabrication techniques, including photolithograpgy, additive manufacturing, and Femtosecond laser processing.

Expertise

  • Reflection Interference Contrast Microscopy (RICM) and Scanning Electron Microscopy
